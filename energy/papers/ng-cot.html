Reading Natural Gas COT Through Time

Henry Hub positioning, 2006–2026

Hao Zhang

Date2026-11-07
MarketNYMEX NG · CFTC 023651
Data1,065 weekly reports
Statusworking paper
Abstract

The CFTC’s weekly Commitments of Traders (COT) report shows how hedge funds and other groups are positioned in NYMEX Henry Hub natural gas. This paper tests whether that positioning helps predict price, using 1,065 weekly reports from June 2006 to November 2026.

Managed-money position changes line up closely with the price move in the same week (correlation 0.494) but not with the following week (−0.005). The report is published three days after the positions are counted, and price moves almost as much in that gap as in the reported week itself. “Funds are at an extreme” does not predict turns: 17 independent extreme episodes in 20 years did no better than the base rate. COT describes who holds exposure. It does not time entries or exits.

1Data

  • Positions: CFTC Disaggregated COT, NAT GAS NYME (code 023651), futures only, Tuesday positions published Friday afternoon. The groups are managed money, producers and merchants, swap dealers, other reportables and non-reportables.
  • Prices: front-month NYMEX natural gas, rolled at expiry into a continuous log-return index. Returns are measured from the first settlement after publication, so no result uses information before it was public.
  • Caution: the Disaggregated report first appeared in September 2009. Earlier history was reconstructed later, so 2006–09 readings were not visible to anyone at the time.

2Positions move with price in the same week

Table 1. Same-week correlation between position change and front-month return, 1,033 weeks.

Position change Correlation Reading
Managed money, net +0.494 Net buying goes with rising price
Managed money, gross long +0.149 Adding longs matters less
Managed money, gross short −0.460 Covering shorts goes with rising price
Other reportables, net −0.510 Moves opposite to funds
Producer/merchant, net −0.261 Hedgers lean against moves

The short side does most of the moving: gross shorts swing a lot more than gross longs (about three times the variance). Among the weeks with the biggest position changes, 43% were driven by short covering and 41% by new shorts, against only 8% by new longs.

3The report is late

Positions are counted on Tuesday and published Friday. From Tuesday to the first settlement after publication:

  • price moved with a standard deviation of 7.0% (log), against 7.4% for the reported week itself;
  • the move went the same way as the reported week only 52% of the time;
  • it fully reversed the reported week’s move 24% of the time.

By the time the report is out, the market has often moved on.

4No forecasting power in the common readings

About 1,030 weeks for flow and raw position, about 875 for percentile. None of the flow or percentile values is statistically distinguishable from zero (HAC |t| ≤ 1.65).

  • Extremes don’t mark turns. De-clustered, there are only 17 independent extreme episodes in 20 years. Fading them did no better than the direction base rate for the same periods, and 73–83% of extremes became more extreme after first being flagged.
  • “Concentrated shorts must squeeze” didn’t hold up. If anything, declines kept going after short positioning got concentrated.
  • Scale matters. Open interest nearly doubled (810,000 contracts in 2006–09 to 1.5 million in 2023–26). A “record” contract count can be ordinary as a share of the market.
  • One exception, fragile. The raw level of managed-money net position had a weak positive link with returns 13 weeks later (about 0.18). That is with the funds, not against them, and it reversed sign in 2023–26.

5Conclusion

COT is a good record of who holds exposure and how it changed. It is a poor timing tool:

  • the same-week link is strong because positions and prices move together;
  • the report arrives late;
  • extremes persist longer than a contrarian reading assumes.

Useful questions to ask of a report:

  1. Is the exposure measured in the right market and at the right time?
  2. Did the change come from longs or shorts?
  3. How far has price moved since Tuesday?
