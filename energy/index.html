01Evening Price Spikes in ERCOT: Net Load, Gas and Battery Output, and the Day-Ahead Premium

working paper · 2026-10-28

Abstract

This paper describes evening price spikes in the ERCOT real-time market using 15-minute settlement prices and generation by fuel from January 2019 to September 2026. Grid-tightness indicators (headroom and Physical Responsive Capability) are strongly associated with the contemporaneous price level but do not lead it. Changes coincide within the same 5-minute dispatch interval, and the tightest decile is followed by a higher price one hour later in only 34% of cases. Spike timing has shifted from 15:00–18:00 in 2019 to 19:00–22:00 in 2025, tracking sunset as solar capacity grew. Across 1,369 evenings since 2023, 8% had a $100/MWh interval when gas and batteries were well below their 30-day highs, against 91% when both were running near them. Low wind at sunset is one of the clearest differences between spike and calm evenings. A simple test of buying sunset hours day-ahead and settling at real-time finds a consistently negative typical outcome. This is consistent with day-ahead prices already including a premium for scarcity risk.

02Where Does the Marginal Cargo Go? Europe's Gas Balance, US Liquefaction Limits and the HH–TTF–JKM Link

working paper · 2026-11

Abstract

This paper follows one chain of reasoning: European gas demand, how Europe covers it, how much LNG Europe needs, how Europe and Asia compete for that LNG, and whether binding US liquefaction capacity limits the transmission of overseas prices to Henry Hub.

A monthly European supply balance built from public data (production + pipeline imports + LNG send-out + net storage withdrawal) comes out about 2.6% below Eurostat’s official consumption figure, close enough to work with. Within that balance, storage does almost all the work of following demand: consumption correlates 0.957 with net storage withdrawal and −0.006 with LNG send-out. LNG doesn’t follow the seasonal demand cycle, and EU-wide, import terminals never ran above 79% of capacity. On prices, Henry Hub is only weakly linked to TTF (weekly correlation 0.09–0.17), while same-month TTF and JKM move almost as one price (0.994). The liquefaction hypothesis points the expected way (HH–TTF correlation 0.05 in months with US plants at ≥ 90% utilisation vs 0.24 otherwise), but only 11 such months exist, too few for a meaningful test. Competition for cargoes is visible in quantities rather than prices: Europe’s share of US cargoes doubled in 2022, and most of the extra volume came from buyers without long-term contracts.

03Reading Natural Gas COT Through Time: Henry Hub Positioning, 2006–2026

working paper · 2026-11-07

Abstract

The CFTC’s weekly Commitments of Traders (COT) report shows how hedge funds and other groups are positioned in NYMEX Henry Hub natural gas. This paper tests whether that positioning helps predict price, using 1,065 weekly reports from June 2006 to November 2026.

Managed-money position changes line up closely with the price move in the same week (correlation 0.494) but not with the following week (−0.005). The report is published three days after the positions are counted, and price moves almost as much in that gap as in the reported week itself. “Funds are at an extreme” does not predict turns: 17 independent extreme episodes in 20 years did no better than the base rate. COT describes who holds exposure. It does not time entries or exits.
