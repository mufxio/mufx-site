The China Gold Tape

A short, visual primer on the futures market the rest of the world can't easily read — and the gold that sits at the center of it. The data is mostly public. The barrier isn't access. It's language.

01 — the word

期货 is just "futures"

A futures contract is a standardized promise: a fixed amount of a thing, at a price agreed today, settled on a date in the future. Strip the romance and that's it — a price, a quantity, a deadline, traded on an exchange that stands in the middle so neither side has to trust the other.

What makes 期货 in China worth a primer of its own isn't the mechanism — it's the ecosystem: the largest, most retail-driven, most self-contained commodity-futures world on earth, walled off behind a closed capital account and a language most Western desks don't read. Gold lives near the middle of it.

03 — the two doors

Gold's two markets: 现货 & 期货

Chinese gold runs on two parallel rails. The SGE is where the physical metal actually moves — bars, deferred contracts, the legal channel through which gold enters the country. The SHFE future is the paper claim layered on top. Read them together and you're reading both the metal and the bet.

04 — why it's its own planet

China gold doesn't trade like the rest of the world

Because the capital account is closed and imports are quota-gated, Chinese gold can't freely arbitrage to the London price. So it forms its own price — and the gap between the two is one of the most honest demand signals in the entire market.

The Shanghai premium

SGE gold trades at a premium or discount to the London price. Premium = China is hungry, importers are paying up to pull metal in. Discount = demand has gone soft. It's a thermometer for Chinese gold appetite — and it's a number, daily.

The 大妈 bid

"Chinese aunties" — 中国大妈 — became shorthand in 2013 when retail buyers swarmed gold on a price dip. Deep, sentiment-driven, jewelry-and-bars retail demand is a structural feature, not a footnote.

De-dollarization

The PBoC has been accumulating gold as a reserve asset, part of a slow pivot away from the dollar. A structural, price-insensitive buyer underneath the market.

The mechanics are different

T+0 (buy and sell same day), a night session (夜盘) that tracks New York/London hours, daily price limits (涨跌停板), and a heavily margined, heavily retail order book.

06 — the signal

The premium is a tape you can read

Picture the Shanghai premium over time. Most of the time it hovers in a narrow band near the London price. Then a demand shock, an import squeeze, or a stress event pulls it wide — and the gap screams before the headline does.

07 — the edge that doesn't transfer

The metagame is in the language

China's futures market is overwhelmingly retail and speculative — and that means the order flow is soaked in sentiment. The forums, the broker chatter, the 大妈 psychology, the way a 期货 crowd piles into 沪金 on a rumor — it's a readable felt-state, a residue of what a whole market is feeling.

A Western desk renting an AI tool can translate the words. It cannot cheaply buy the thing underneath: which print matters, what the crowd actually means, who's really bidding. That layer is context and trust, and it doesn't transfer through a translation API.

AI killed the cost of reading the Chinese tape. It didn't touch the cost of understanding it. That gap is the whole corner.
08 — where you go in

The un-contested corner

Not a job. A corner: the China-gold read-through, in a language the desks that move the world price can't read natively. The price is public (SGE, the premium series). The mechanics are learnable (this page). The wedge is the part you can't fake — the native read on what the corridor is feeling.

You don't need a balance sheet to occupy the information layer of this market. You need the tape, the language, and the discipline to score yourself honestly. Two of the three, you already have.

Start by watching one number: the Shanghai premium. Ask it, every day, the only question that matters — what did the sink just do?
